MiniMARS

Type a MIPS program, assemble it, and run it. Step one instruction at a time and watch the PC, the registers and memory change.

Little-endian memory, like MARS. Execution starts at the first instruction. Saved programs stay in this browser; Download keeps a copy as a file.

1

Branches and jumps take effect immediately. MiniMARS does not use MIPS delay slots.

Supported instructions


Registers can be written by name or number, such as $t0 or $8. $zero stays zero. $fp is $s8. Labels and # comments work. Arithmetic wraps and does not trap. Branches and jumps have no delay slot.






InstructionFormWhat it does



addadd $t0, $t1, $t2Signed sum. Wraps; it does not trap.


subsub $t0, $t1, $t2Subtract. Wraps; it does not trap.


andand $t0, $t1, $t2Bitwise and.


oror $t0, $t1, $t2Bitwise or.


sltslt $t0, $t1, $t21 if $t1 is signed-less than $t2, otherwise 0.


addiaddi $t0, $t1, -4Add a signed 16-bit immediate.


subisubi $t0, $t1, 4Pseudo. Assembles as addi with the immediate negated.


andiandi $t0, $t1, 0xffAnd with a 16-bit immediate, zero-extended.


oriori $t0, $t1, 0xffOr with a 16-bit immediate, zero-extended.


sltislti $t0, $t1, 101 if $t1 is signed-less than the immediate.


luilui $t0, 0x1001Load the immediate into the upper 16 bits.


lwlw $t0, 4($sp)Load the word at $sp + 4. The address must be a multiple of 4.


swsw $t0, 0($t1)Store the word in $t0. The address must be a multiple of 4.


beqbeq $t0, $t1, loopBranch if equal. No delay slot.


bnebne $t0, $zero, loopBranch if not equal. No delay slot.


jj loopJump. No delay slot.


jaljal funcJump and save the next instruction’s address in $ra.


jrjr $raJump to the address in a register.


syscallsyscallDo the service numbered in $v0. See the table below.


nopnopDo nothing.


lili $t0, 5Pseudo. Load a number. A large value is lui then ori, and Step walks both.


lala $a0, msgPseudo. Load a label’s address with lui and ori.


movemove $a0, $t0Pseudo. Copy a register (add with $zero).


addu subu addiuaddu $t0, $t1, $t2Same wrap as add, sub and addi. The u does not change the bits here, because nothing traps.


xor nor xorixor $t0, $t1, $t2Bitwise xor, nor, and xor with a zero-extended immediate.


notnot $t0, $t1Pseudo. nor with $zero.


sltu sltiusltu $t0, $t1, $t2Unsigned compare. sltiu sign-extends its immediate first.


sll srl srasll $t0, $t1, 4Shift left, logical right, or arithmetic right by 0..31.


sllv srlv sravsllv $t0, $t1, $t2Same shifts, with the amount in the low 5 bits of a register.


mulmul $t0, $t1, $t2Low 32 bits of the signed product. HI and LO stay as they are.


mult multumult $t0, $t164-bit signed or unsigned product in HI and LO.


div divudiv $t0, $t1Quotient in LO, remainder in HI. Three operands also copy LO into the first register. Division by zero stops the program.


mfhi mflomflo $t0Copy HI or LO into a register. mthi and mtlo go the other way.


lb lbu lh lhulb $t0, 0($t1)Load a byte or halfword. Without u, the value is sign-extended. A halfword address must be even.


sb shsb $t0, 0($t1)Store the low 8 or 16 bits. A halfword address must be even.


beqz bnezbeqz $t0, loopPseudo. Branch when the register is zero, or is not.


blt ble bgt bgeblt $t0, $t1, loopPseudo. Signed compare, then a branch. Uses $at. bltu, bleu, bgtu and bgeu compare unsigned.


bltz bgez bgtz blezbgez $t0, loopBranch on the sign of one register. No delay slot.








Floating point


Values are IEEE 754. The coprocessor does not trap: division by zero produces an infinity, and a NaN comparison is false. cond next to the PC is the floating-point condition flag.






InstructionFormWhat it does



lwc1 swc1lwc1 $f0, 0($t0)Load or store one single. The address must be a multiple of 4.


ldc1 sdc1ldc1 $f0, 0($t0)Load or store a double in an even register and the next one, little-endian.


add.s sub.s mul.s div.smul.s $f4, $f0, $f2Single-precision arithmetic. .d is the double form, and both registers must be even.


mov.s abs.s neg.sneg.s $f2, $f0Copy, clear the sign, or flip the sign. .d does the same for a double.


cvt.s.w cvt.w.scvt.s.w $f2, $f0Convert a word and a single, either way. cvt.d.w, cvt.w.d, cvt.d.s and cvt.s.d cover the other pairs. A value that does not fit in a word stops the program.


mtc1 mfc1mtc1 $t0, $f0Move raw bits between a general register and an FP register.


c.eq.s c.lt.s c.le.sc.eq.s $f0, $f2Set cond to 1 or 0. .d compares doubles.


bc1t bc1fbc1t loopBranch when cond is 1, or when it is 0. No delay slot.








Data


.text and .data switch sections, and either may be given an address. .globl is accepted and ignored. A label on a directive lands on the first byte that directive writes, after alignment.






DirectiveExampleWhat it stores



.word.word 3, msg32-bit values, aligned to 4. A label stores its address.


.half.half -1, 0x800116-bit values, aligned to 2.


.byte.byte 10, 'A'Bytes. A number, a character or a one-character string.


.ascii .asciiz.asciiz "hi\n"The characters. .asciiz adds the terminating 0.


.float .double.float 1.5IEEE single or double. Use .word for raw bits.


.space.space 16That many zero bytes.


.align.align 2Align to 2 to that power. .align 2 is a word.








Syscalls


Put the service number in $v0 before syscall. Any other number stops the program. A run also stops after 10,000 steps. Ctrl+Enter runs the program. Files exist only for this run: nothing is written to disk.






$v0UsesEffect



1$a0Print the signed integer in $a0.


2$f12Print the single in $f12.


3$f12Print the double in $f12.


4$a0Print the null-terminated string at $a0.


5result in $v0Read one integer from Console input into $v0.


6result in $f0Read one number into $f0.


7result in $f0Read one number into the double $f0.


8$a0, $a1Read a line into the buffer at $a0. $a1 is the buffer length, including the terminating 0.


9$a0, result in $v0Allocate $a0 bytes on the heap and return the address. The size is rounded up to a word.


10Exit.


11$a0Print the low 8 bits of $a0 as a character.


12result in $v0Read one character into $v0.


13$a0, $a1Open the file named at $a0. $a1 is 0 to read, 1 to create, or 9 to append. The descriptor comes back in $v0, or −1 if a read cannot find the file.


14$a0, $a1, $a2Read up to $a2 bytes from descriptor $a0 into $a1. $v0 is the count.


15$a0, $a1, $a2Write $a2 bytes from $a1 to descriptor $a0.


16$a0Close descriptor $a0.


17$a0Exit with the code in $a0.
